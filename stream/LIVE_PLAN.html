What happens today

The experiment in one minute

  • 10:00 Two quantum random numbers are drawn, 24 bits each (6 hex characters): one from the ANU quantum random number service, one from 24 qubits on an IBM Quantum processor.
  • Each number is sealed with a secret 256-bit salt and two hashes (SHA-256 and SHA3-512). Only the hashes are posted on X. The numbers stay hidden.
  • 13:00:00 The NIST Randomness Beacon publishes a new random value that nobody can predict. The target is its first 6 hex characters.
  • A draw is a HIT only if its 6 characters equal the target exactly. After the target is public, the numbers and salts are revealed and anyone can check them.
1 in 16,777,216
chance of a hit, per draw
≈ 1 in 8,388,608
chance that at least one draw hits
2−48 ≈ 3.6 × 10−15
chance that both draws hit

Expected outcome: INCONCLUSIVE (no hit). A single miss refutes nothing; a hit would be treated first as a possible error.

T1 · 13:00:00 CEST · 11:00:00 UTC

The target

  • The target pulse is the first NIST Randomness Beacon 2.0 pulse stamped at or after 13:00:00 CEST (11:00:00 UTC) on 12 October 2026.
  • The target is the first 6 characters of its outputValue, in uppercase. NIST is the only source; there is no backup source.
  • Anyone can open the same pulse, now or years from now (this page shows "404 not found" until 13:00):

https://beacon.nist.gov/beacon/2.0/pulse/time/1791802800000

Secondary analysis (separate, not live-streamed)

  • Three later pulses are compared with the values already made public: N2 ≈ 14:00 today, N3 ≈ Tue 13 Oct 13:00, N4 ≈ Mon 19 Oct 13:00 (CEST).
  • They ask whether the beacon itself can be influenced. They never change the primary result.

Transparency

What you will see, and how to check it

On screen

  • Every command and its full output
  • The commitment hashes and the X posts with their times
  • The NIST pulse on NIST's own website
  • At the reveal: both numbers, both salts, the result

Not shown before the reveal

  • The two numbers and their salts: never displayed, stored only in private files and backed up offline
  • API keys and the IBM job result page are never shown

Check it yourself

  • Instructions: VERIFY.md in github.com/hanzunye/handshake-001
  • One command recomputes every hash, fetches the target from NIST and recomputes the result:
    python3 handshake.py verify --anu-sha256 … --ibm-sha3-512 …
  • Use the hashes as posted on X, not the copies in the repository.
